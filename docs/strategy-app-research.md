# Strategy software: 20 applications, 15 views, and what it means for OrgCue

Research date: 9 November 2026. Prepared for the OrgCue corporate strategy MVP.

## Read this first

- **Most evidence is vendor material.** Feature lists, outcome numbers and ROI claims come from vendors' own sites, help centres, press releases and customer stories. Only a few figures are measured by a third party. Treat every number in this report as "the vendor says".
- **Some pages could not be opened directly.** The research environment blocked several vendor sites. Where that happened the findings rest on search-result excerpts of the official pages, and the source lists say so. Anything we could not confirm is marked *unverified*.
- **No prices.** This report deliberately leaves out pricing.

## Summary

1. **The market has three camps.**
   - **Strategy and performance management** tools model the strategy itself: scorecards, strategy maps, reporting cycles. Examples: ClearPoint, Spider Impact, Envisio, AchieveIt, Corporater, StrategyBlocks, OnStrategy.
   - **OKR and goal platforms** focus on alignment and weekly check-ins. Examples: Perdoo, Mooncamp, Tability, Betterworks, Asana Goals.
   - **Strategic portfolio management (SPM)** tools connect strategy to money, capacity and delivery. Examples: Planview, i-nexus.
   - The leaders are converging on all three at once: WorkBoard (which bought Quantive in May 2025), Cascade, Profit.co and Workpath.
2. **Value is created by closing the loop between plan and work.** Every successful product does four things: links objectives to the work and measures behind them; pulls status automatically from the systems where work happens; runs a cadence of check-ins and reviews; and turns the result into a pack leaders read. AI is now used mainly to draft plans, summarise status and flag risk.
3. **Value is presented as saved reporting time and faster decisions.** The most common outcome claim is reporting time saved (75 to 90% in ClearPoint and Envisio customer stories). Next come faster or better decisions (Planview, WorkBoard) and higher participation in goal setting (Profit.co, Betterworks, AchieveIt). Few vendors publish a measured ROI study.
4. **The views that matter most are few.** Every leader has a hierarchy or alignment view, a status heat map or scorecard, a timeline or roadmap, and a review or board pack. Canvases (Blue Ocean, business model canvas) are mostly left to whiteboard tools. Hoshin X-matrices appear only in specialists (i-nexus, Profit.co, KaiNexus, WorkBoard's bowlers).
5. **Gaps OrgCue can use.**
   - One product that covers strategy formulation (canvas, SWOT, business model), the framework (map, scorecard, OKR, X-matrix) and execution (roadmap, prioritisation, heat map) on one data model.
   - Ease of use, which reviewers repeatedly say the enterprise tools lack. Planview reviewers cite a steep learning curve and a reliance on Power BI for reports.
   - Honest "exceptions only" board packs, which governance experts recommend but few tools produce by default.

## How the leaders present value

- **Board and review packs** generated from live data: ClearPoint Briefing Books, Spider Impact Briefings, Corporater's "Board-ready report", WorkBoard Biz Reviews, Cascade Snapshot Reports, OnStrategy's Strategy Review PowerPoint.
- **Executive briefs** that answer a few questions. WorkBoard's Executive Brief asks "Are we on track? / Where is money at risk? / What needs me? / What needs a look?"
- **Public dashboards** for the public sector, auto-synced from the plan, from ClearPoint, Envisio and AchieveIt. Envisio adds a publish toggle on each item and Google Analytics.
- **AI summaries** on top of every report, for example Envisio's accomplishments, roadblocks and recommendations, and Cascade's "Wins and Challenges".
- **Outcome claims** (all vendor-reported):
  - **Reporting time:**
    - City of St. Albert: report creation from about 100 hours to 25 per cycle (ClearPoint).
    - Southern Ohio Medical Center: 40 hours a month to 15 to 20 minutes (ClearPoint).
    - Avery Dennison: 80% less MBR preparation time (WorkBoard).
    - Intuitive Surgical: 75% less executive review time (Profit.co).
    - Envisio: "90% less time spent creating reports".
  - **Decisions:** "25% faster" decisions at Santander UK (Planview, AWS partner brief). Cascade claims a "$20 Mn average reduction in spend on misaligned projects" but does not attribute it.
  - **Participation:**
    - KeHE: goal participation from under 10% to 70% (Betterworks).
    - Toyota Connected: goal-setting participation from 60% to 80% (Profit.co).
    - A G2 reviewer: update compliance from 20% to 90% with email updates (AchieveIt).
  - **Caution:** ClearPoint's own blog admits that an earlier "83% fewer reporting hours" figure was a model calculation, not a measurement.

## What this means for OrgCue

1. **Lead with one connected model.** Pillars, objectives, measures, initiatives, projects, risks and people should be one data set, shown through many views. The 15 strategy pages in the MVP all draw on one sample strategy for "MosaicX" to show exactly this.
2. **Make alignment a number.** Show an alignment score, and flag teams, projects and red measures that have no link to the strategy. The organisation alignment and review pages do this today.
3. **Default to exceptions.** The strategy review page answers WorkBoard-style questions and lists only objectives at risk or off track. Governance guidance agrees: "three well-explained exceptions beat a twenty-row status table" (Board Intelligence).
4. **Cover the whole cycle in one place.** Formulate with the canvas, business model canvas and SWOT/TOWS. Frame it with the map, scorecard, OKRs, OGSM and X-matrix. Execute with the roadmap, prioritisation, heat map and organisation view. Review with the quarterly pack. Few competitors cover all four.
5. **Next steps after the MVP:**
   - Connectors for live data.
   - Reporting cycles with reminders and a period lock.
   - AI summaries that are clearly labelled and reviewed by a person before they reach a board.
   - Snapshots of each period for audit.
   - A public dashboard option for public-sector buyers.

Common mistakes the research flags, which the pages try to avoid:
- strategy maps with too many boxes and arrows nobody can defend;
- OKRs cascaded mechanically;
- risk matrices treated as precise;
- SWOT lists that never become strategies (hence the TOWS grid);
- portfolio scores with equal weights (hence the sliders);
- board packs that list everything instead of the exceptions.

## Method

- Five research passes ran in parallel: four covered five applications each, and one covered the 15 framework views.
- Each pass used web search, official product pages, help centres, release notes and review sites.
- Direct page fetching was blocked for several vendor domains. In those cases the passes relied on search excerpts of the same official pages; the source lists say so where it applies.
- All outcome numbers above are as reported by the vendor or its customers unless a third party is named.
